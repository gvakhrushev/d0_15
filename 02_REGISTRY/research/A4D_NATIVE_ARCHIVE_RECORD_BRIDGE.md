## 5. The coordinatewise Role inverse limit has a different exact type

For the actual phase projections `Fin(L+1)→Fin L`, the fibre over 0 is
`{0,L}` and every positive `m<L` has unique lift m. Let `(x_L)_{L≥2}` be
an infinite compatible coordinate history.

If no coordinate is positive, it is the all-zero history. Otherwise at its
first positive value m, unique lifting forces `x_L=m` for every later L.
Its earlier values are forced backwards: zero for `L≤m`, m for `L>m`
(including m=1, which is positive at the initial level).

The coordinate inverse limit is thus the one-point compactification of
the positive integers: m labels the eventually constant history m, and
∞ labels the all-zero history. Histories with m→∞ converge to zero in
every fixed finite coordinate. The four-coordinate Role inverse limit is
the fourth power of this countable compact space, hence countable and
scattered.

Consequently no compatible family of finite surjections from this **site
address** tower to a record-prefix tower with infinitely many two-point
`B_i` can exist, even after cofinal reindexing. Such maps would induce a
surjection of inverse limits: for each target history, its finite lifting
cylinders are nonempty nested compact sets, so have an infinite lift.
The target contains a Cantor set and is uncountable, while the native
coordinate address limit is countable.

This is not a physical field-state NO-GO. Fields on a finite site carrier
are a different domain and may have many degrees of freedom. Nor have we
proved infinitely many non-singleton `B_i` for the fixed experiment. The
result precisely rules out identifying the coordinate address tower with
the universal golden record support. The construction in Sections 2--4
