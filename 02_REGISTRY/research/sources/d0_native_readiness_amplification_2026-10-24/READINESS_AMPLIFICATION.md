## 2. Exact equal-phase recursion on this entire input subspace

Work first in the complexification of the real carrier. Choose the same
exactly native phase `t=c+i s=exp(iβ)` for both predicates, where

\[
 s>0,\qquad \tfrac12<c<\tfrac{17}{32},\qquad
 a=2(1-c),\qquad b=2c-1=1-a.
\]

Thus `0<b<1/16` and `15/16<a<1`. Section 5 constructs this phase by
finite integer Golden powers and exact algebraic inequalities. Set

\[
 R_{\rm in}=I+(t-1)P_{\rm base},
 \qquad R_R=I+(t-1)\Pi_R.
\]

For `A_0=A` define, in matrix multiplication order,

\[
 A_{r+1}=A_rR_{\rm in}A_r^\dagger R_RA_r.              (A3)
\]

The chronological order is therefore `A_r; R_R; A_r†; R_in; A_r`.
Let `ε=1-γ` and split `A J_0=v_g+v_b` by `Π_R`. From (A1),

\[
 (A P_{\rm base}A^\dagger)(t v_g+v_b)
       =(t\gamma+\varepsilon)(v_g+v_b).
\]

For every unit-modulus `t`, `(t-1)²=-a t`. The bad component of (A3)
is therefore multiplied by `t(1-aγ)=t(b+aε)`. The good component is
multiplied by the same scalar on every input,
`g(γ)=t+(t-1)(tγ+ε)`. Consequently

\[
 \Pi_R A_1J_0=g(\gamma)\Pi_RA J_0,
 \qquad \varepsilon_1=h(\varepsilon)
       :=\varepsilon(b+a\varepsilon)^2,
 \qquad \gamma_1=1-h(1-\gamma).                        (A4)
\]

Unitarity and the orthogonal good/bad split prove the displayed success
law. Since `0<b+aε<1` for `0≤ε<1`, success remains positive and increases.
This proves (A1) again for `A_1`, with the same successful isometry up
to one scalar phase. Inductively define `F_r(γ)=1-h^r(1-γ)`; then

\[
 P_{\rm base}A_r^\dagger\Pi_RA_rP_{\rm base}
       =\gamma_rP_{\rm base},
 \qquad \gamma_r=F_r(\gamma),\qquad
 \varepsilon_r=h^r(\varepsilon_0).                    (A5)
\]

The normalized successful operation is the original complete logical
operation and retained successful transcript, times a global phase
depending on `γ`. It preserves all algorithm-control coherences **inside
that operation**, unknown DATA and oldRIGHT correlations. This is not a
promise that a subsequently **controlled** call of `A_r` has the same
relative phase as an idle branch. No such controlled amplification call
is required: (A3) wraps the top-level whole §7s forward programme.

At the semantic level the recursion uses `L=3^r` occurrences of base
`A/A†`, namely `(L+1)/2`
forward and `(L-1)/2` reverse occurrences, and `L-1` phase predicates.
These are oracle-call counts only, **not** `3^r` raw stages. If one
current raw word has counts `(n_G,n_CX,n_I)` and `n_off` of its G
letters are away from the source port, its compiled exact inverse has
counts `(n_G,n_CX+6 n_off,3 n_I+4 n_G)`. If the two fixed predicate
phase words together have counts `(p_G,p_CX,p_I)`, one recursive step has

\[
 n'_G=3n_G+p_G,\qquad
 n'_{CX}=3n_{CX}+6n_{\rm off}+p_{CX},\qquad
 n'_I=5n_I+4n_G+p_I.                                  (A5a)
\]

Thus the literal inverse/compiler accounting includes `5^r` source-I
growth. Use the actual expanded-word counter and clock; no oracle-call
count replaces it. Registers and spin are reused coherently, not freshly
cloned or reset at each recursive call.

More explicitly define the weighted inventory `W=5 n_G+n_CX+n_I`.
The inverse counts above give `W(inv word)≤3 W(word)`. If `C_phase`
is the fixed weighted count of both phase words, then
`W_r≤5 W_(r-1)+C_phase`, hence

\[
 N_r\le W_r\le
 5^rW_0+(5^r-1)C_{\rm phase}/4.                        (A5b)
\]

Here `N_r` is the **literal** primitive-stage count. The same work is
reused at each call; only the one phase rebit was added. Its input blank,
all original coherent work and every actual clock mode stay counted.

## 3. Realification, the two input projectors, and immutable history

Use one fresh ordinary rebit `h`, initially `|0_h⟩`, to realify the

 \mathcal R(P)=I_h\otimes(I-P)+R(\beta)\otimes P.        (A6)

  Hence `P_book ≤ P_ref`. It is still computed from the actual input

Using `P_book` in place of `P_ref` inside a presumed complex phase would

## 4. Uniform suppression of the actual first source, not only failure

Fix a declared collar with `γ≥γ_min>0` and put `ε_max=1-γ_min<1`.
The scalar polynomial in (A4) has derivatives

\[
 h'(\varepsilon)=(b+a\varepsilon)(b+3a\varepsilon),
 \qquad h''(\varepsilon)=4ab+6a^2\varepsilon.            (A7)
\]

Let `λ_0=(b+a ε_max)²<1`. Monotonicity and (A4) give
`ε_r≤ε_max λ_0^r`, uniformly on that collar. Choose a finite warm-up
integer `R` such that `ε_max λ_0^R≤1/48`. Thereafter `a<1`, `b<1/16`
give the two uniform bounds

\[
 h(\varepsilon_r)/\varepsilon_r<1/144,\qquad
 0<h'(\varepsilon_r)<(1/12)(1/8)=1/96.
\]

The failure ratio at zero is read by continuity. Put `κ=1/96`.
Before warm-up, `h'≤3` on `[0,1]`. The chain rule consequently gives

\[
 0<F_r'(\gamma)\le3^R\kappa^{\,r-R}
 \quad(r\ge R),\qquad
 |D\log\gamma_r|\le
 {3^R\kappa^{r-R}\over\gamma_{\min}}|D\gamma|.           (A7a)
\]

This is a uniform **first-source** estimate. Merely showing
`γ_r→1` would not suffice. `γ_r≥γ≥γ_min`, so normalization has introduced
no unknown inverse success factor. The actual `γ_min` may nevertheless
be extremely small and is not assumed uniform in mesh, bank count or
factory count. The warm-up can therefore be enormous.

A uniform C2 bound is also available when the base comparison is C2.
Write `D_r=sup F_r'` and `B_r=sup |F_r''|`. Since `h''≤6` on `[0,1]`,
`D_R≤3^R` and `B_R≤9^R-3^R`. For `n≥1`, differentiating the recurrence
and summing its positive bounds gives

\[
 B_{R+n}\le\kappa^n B_R+
 6D_R^2\kappa^{n-1}{1-\kappa^n\over1-\kappa}
 \longrightarrow0.                                   (A7b)
\]

Hence `D²γ_r=F_r''(γ)Dγ⊗Dγ+F_r'(γ)D²γ` also tends uniformly to zero
on collars with bounded base first and second derivatives. This does
not assert derivative bounds for independently moving controller,
source-spin placement or cut parameters absent from that comparison.

### The whole readiness operator has a duration-counted first-source bound

There is a stronger statement for **fixed input and READY projectors**.
Let `C=A_r J_ref` be the complete real isometry on the enlarged unknown
input leg, so `C†C=I` and `C†Π_R C=γ_r I` at the base programme. With
`Q_C=I-CC†`,

\[
 (Q_C\Pi_R C)^\dagger(Q_C\Pi_R C)
       =\gamma_r(1-\gamma_r)I.
\]

For an arbitrary unitary matrix variation of this **same** programme,
`C†D C` is skew adjoint. Its contribution to `D(C†Π_R C)` cancels
because the base response is scalar. The remaining two terms yield

\[
 \|D\Gamma_R\|_{op}
 \le2\sqrt{\gamma_r\varepsilon_r}\,\|D A_r\|_{op},
 \qquad
 \|D\log\Gamma_R\|_{op}
 \le2\sqrt{\varepsilon_r/\gamma_r}\,\|D A_r\|_{op}.     (A7c)
\]

This is an operator estimate, uniform over unknown DATA/reference inputs,
not merely a scalar probability derivative. The logarithm derivative is
evaluated at `Γ_R=γ_r I`. It requires no derivative assumption on a
normalized successful channel.

For a declared smooth comparison of the actual primitive factors,
write `D g_j=K_j g_j` with `K_j` skew adjoint. Product differentiation gives

\[
 \|D A_r\|_{op}\le\sum_{j=1}^{N_r}\|K_j\|_{op}
 \le\sqrt{N_r}\,\|K\|_2
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad N_r\|K\|_\infty.                (A7d)

Thus the log-readiness operator covector norm is at most
`2 sqrt(N_r ε_r/γ_r)` for the indicated Euclidean generator norm,
or `2 N_r sqrt(ε_r/γ_r)` for the per-stage supremum norm. The latter
counts the entire duration. By (A5b), and by
`ε_(R+n)≤ε_R 144^(-n)`,

\[
 N_{R+n}^2\varepsilon_{R+n}
 \le C^2 25^R\varepsilon_R(25/144)^n\longrightarrow0,  (A7e)
\]

where `C=W_0+C_phase/4` is an actual fixed inventory bound. Since
`γ_r≥γ_min`, both covector estimates tend to zero. Exponential literal
inverse growth has therefore not been hidden in an oracle-call norm.

This does not declare a freely adjustable smooth pulse at every fixed
native letter. The generator norm must come from an actual comparison
or proved native-edit lift. For two actual endpoints, putting
`Δ=C_new-C` gives the exact safe remainder estimate

\[
 \|\Gamma_{R,new}-\gamma_r I\|_{op}
 \le2\sqrt{\gamma_r\varepsilon_r}\,\|\Delta\|_{op}
       +\|\Delta\|_{op}^2.                            (A7f)

It follows by using `C†Δ+Δ†C=-Δ†Δ` and splitting
`Π_R C=γ_r C+Q_CΠ_R C`. The quadratic term is retained: an arbitrary

Moving `Π_R` or the input projector adds its own source terms. Nor does

## 5. One exact native phase serves both predicates

The π/3 fixed-point identity is a useful guide: setting `a=1,b=0`
would give `ε_next=ε³`. It is **not required here**. In particular no
`sqrt(3)` phase primitive is introduced into the raw alphabet.

For ordinary control bits `x=(x_1,...,x_m)` and a desired string `d`,

\[
 2^m\mathbf1_{x=d}
 =\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,...,m\}}
          (-1)^{S\cdot d}(-1)^{S\cdot x}.              (A9)
\]

Let `C_S` be the product of existing `CX(x_j,h)`, `j∈S`, targeting the
fresh phase rebit. Conjugation changes `G_h^k` to
`G_h^{(-1)^(S·x)k}`. The commuting product
`C_S G_h^{(-1)^(S·d)k_P} C_S` consequently equals identity off `x=d`
and `G_h^{2^m k_P}` on that predicate. All columns and all controls are
retained, including failed factory columns. Both all-zero input and
all-one READY predicates are covered; no free X or known-one-at-input
assumption is needed. Negative powers use the actual source-I inverse.

Let `K_max` be the larger number of ordinary control bits in `P_ref`
and `Π_R`. Choose one positive integer `k`, and for a predicate with
`m=K_P` controls set `k_P=2^(K_max-K_P)k`. Both words then implement
**exactly the same** rebit rotation

\[
 R(\beta)=G^{\,2^{K_{\max}}k},\qquad
 c=(G^{\,2^{K_{\max}}k})_{00},\qquad
 s=(G^{\,2^{K_{\max}}k})_{10}.                          (A10)
\]

The owned irrational Golden-angle density guarantees a finite positive
`k` with `s>0` and `1/2<c<17/32`. An integer enumeration can test these
strict inequalities **exactly in the real algebraic field** of the raw
matrices. The open interval and density prove termination. There is no
transcendental angle target, tolerance oracle or fitted physical phase.
Every matrix entry remains in the existing field `Q(A_G)`, where the
Golden gate coefficient `A_G>0` satisfies `A_G⁴+A_G²-1=0`. This is
separate from both `a=2(1-c)` in (A4) and the inherited report gain
`a_0` in (A8).

The rebit and every predicate control are distinct from the original
source-spin wires. Spin is not selected or addressed as a Boolean
control. The source port may be dirty or also be a predicate control:
each completed routed negative-G block is exactly the required rebit
inverse tensor identity on that port and on the spin. Intermediate
spin/port interactions remain in the counted schedule.

For `m≥1` the parity conjugations contribute exactly `m 2^m` CX before
negative-power inverse/routing expansions. Every `|k_P|` rotation
multiplicity and its source-I calls are included in the literal count.
The no-control case is simply a whole-rebit Golden power. Adjoint
calls in (A3) use the exact reverse of the already selected raw word;
no separately selected reverse phase is substituted. The recursion's
physical inventory is therefore (A5a), evaluated on actual expanded
words, and there is **no phase approximation remainder**.

The finite-accuracy §7s logical query remains its existing actual query;
no accuracy claim about that target was strengthened by calling the
readiness phases exact. What is exact is amplification of its actual
all-input READY isometry.

## 6. Application boundary

The all-input hypothesis (A1) holds for the declared typed §7s source
and corrected-factory programme. Native equal-phase recursion therefore
suppresses the specific genuine §7t resource/readiness source, including
its derivative and its actual finite-code divided differences. It does
so while retaining all source preparations, failed columns, new rebit,
reused source spin, protected unknown DATA and immutable oldRIGHT.

A common completed-operator comparison for the §7t endpoints is explicit:
on `c0=1` replace its two completed `W_(Lcell,k)` blocks by `G(α(u))`,
and keep their actual fixed `G^Lcell` complements on `c0=0`, with
`α(u)=arccos(u)/2`. On `|u|≤ρ<1`,
`||D_u A||≤1/sqrt(1-ρ²)`. These are genuine all-column endpoint
identities and a smooth finite-matrix comparison, not a differentiable
finite-word selector or an identification of different raw clock
histories. Alternatively use the fully algebraic endpoint statement
(A8a). The amplification phases and recursion depth remain fixed on
each chosen stencil; finer stencils have their actual new absolute
inventories and clocks.

This does not establish stationarity under every newly introduced
controller/phase-word edit, fix arbitrary external source variations,
or select the amplified programme as the physical law. Source-I physical
availability, independent frame/cut/clock equations, spatial scene
identification and stationary refinement retain their declared scope.
The outer Book echo always uses the actual full-column inverse, so no
thermal mode is discarded and no different heat anchor is silently
subtracted. There is no unknown external seed, measured transcript or
immutable history being uncomputed. The result is a positive consumer
of one named readiness row, not complete native GR. `GR_closed=false`.
