## 5. A universal C1 refinement tail without positive-probability floors

The original row co-completeness and `0<=Delta<=I` give

\[
0\le\Theta^*I=2t(\Delta-\Delta^2)\le\frac t2 I=p^2I.
\]

Let `alpha<=p^2` bound this CP contraction and let `eta` bound the induced Hermitian trace norm of `DTheta`. Put `R=||D Omega_D||_1`. For the scalar quantity `a_n=Tr Theta^n(Omega_D)`,

\[
|Da_n|\le\alpha^nR+n\alpha^{n-1}\eta.
\]

For correlated retained input, this uses its DATA marginal; therefore an unamplified DATA bound suffices for this particular scalar correction. Alternatively the directly computable Stinespring choice

\[
\eta=2\sqrt{\alpha\,\left\|\sum_j(DM_j)^*DM_j\right\|}
\]

is also safe under arbitrary reference amplification.

Writing `s=q0^2+q1^2` and `r_c=alpha p`, the binary entropy bounds are

\[
f_n\le(\log2)s p^{n-2},\qquad g_N\le(\log2)p^{N-1}.
\]

The infinite matched series `Delta I_infinity=sum_(n>=2) f_n Tr Theta^n Omega_D` therefore converges, and explicit geometric sums yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\Delta I_\infty-\Delta I_N|
&\le(\log2)r_c^{N-1}
 \left[\alpha+\frac{s\alpha^2}{1-r_c}\right],\\
|D(\Delta I_\infty-\Delta I_N)|
&\le(\log2)r_c^{N-1}\left\{
R\left[\alpha+\frac{s\alpha^2}{1-r_c}\right]
+\eta\left[N+s\alpha\left(\frac{N+1}{1-r_c}
                         +\frac{r_c}{(1-r_c)^2}\right)\right]\right\}.
\end{aligned}\tag{14}
\]

Using only `alpha<=p^2<2/5`, `r_c<=p^3<1/4`, `s<1`, `log2<1` gives the simpler universal bounds

\[
\boxed{
|\Delta I_\infty-\Delta I_N|<4^{-(N-1)},\qquad
|D(\Delta I_\infty-\Delta I_N)|
\le4^{-(N-1)}[R+(2N+1)\eta].}\tag{15}
\]

In particular the N=64 factors are exactly `2^-126` and `2^-126 [R+129 eta]`. Uniform C1 convergence holds on any common chart with bounded R and eta; no fine-label probability floor, stationary input or mixing gap is needed for this refinement term.

Identifying the series with a complete actual uncapped stopping instrument still uses the owned transience/completeness assumption. Equations (14)–(15) are **not** a tail bound for the complete merged entropy, the actual stopping clock, or the other cost terms.
