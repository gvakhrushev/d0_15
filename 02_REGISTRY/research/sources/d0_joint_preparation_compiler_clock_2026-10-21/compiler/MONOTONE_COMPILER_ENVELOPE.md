## 4. Finite lower envelope: full stationary equivalence, including ties

For a finite, genuinely admitted alternative set J define

    V(s)=min_(j in J) [B_j+g_j s],
    S_min(y)=V(F(y)),
    g_min=min_j g_j>0,   g_max=max_j g_j.

For s2>s1, comparison with a minimizing line at s1 and at s2 gives

    g_min(s2-s1) <= V(s2)-V(s1) <= g_max(s2-s1).           (4)

Hence V is strictly increasing and locally Lipschitz. This proof uses
the real B_j; setup costs cannot be deleted before the comparison.

If DF(y*)=0, then F(y*+v)-F(y*)=o(||v||). The Lipschitz bound proves
S_min(y*+v)-S_min(y*)=o(||v||), so S_min is Frechet differentiable at y*
with derivative zero, EVEN if V has a kink at F(y*).

If DF(y*) is nonzero, choose v with DF(y*)[v]<0. For small t>0,
F(y*+tv)-F(y*)=t DF(y*)[v]+o(t)<0. Equation (4), with the arguments
in increasing order, gives

    S_min(y*+tv)-S_min(y*)
       <= g_min [t DF(y*)[v]+o(t)] <0.                   (5)

There is a strict first-order descent, so a tie cannot manufacture a
stationary point. In particular the differentiable critical sets agree;
equivalently the finite envelope's generalized first covectors are
positive multiples of DF and cannot contain zero when DF is nonzero.

Strict increase also preserves EVERY local minimum, local maximum,
strict extremum and saddle of F: compare F(y) to F(y*) before applying V.
This is stronger than merely a necessary Euler condition at a minimum.
