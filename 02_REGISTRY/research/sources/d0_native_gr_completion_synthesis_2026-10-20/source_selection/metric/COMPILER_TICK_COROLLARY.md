## Exact proof

Put

    q1=p², q2=4p³, L=log_phi 4, A=4+L, D2=q2 A-5.

The Golden identities give q2>q1>0 and p^6+q2=1. The endpoint divergences
are D(q0)=1, D(q1)=0, D(q2)=D2. Thus strict superiority of k=2 to k=0 and
k=1 respectively requires

    delta q2 > 2 ell_G + D2 - 1,                    (C1)
    delta(q2-q1) > ell_G + D2.                     (C2)

Strict positivity of its full first-source coefficient requires

    delta < A.                                     (C3)

**Any ell_G>=3 is impossible.** Equation (C1) implies

    delta q2 > 2 ell_G+D2-1 >= D2+5 = A q2.

Since q2>0, this implies delta>A, contradicting (C3). This exclusion does not
even use integrality of delta.

**ell_G=2 admits no integer delta.** Direct Golden algebra gives

    2+D2-6(q2-q1) = q2(L-11/4).                    (C4)

Moreover phi^11=89 phi+55, and 1<phi<2 implies

    phi^11 < 233 < 256 = 4^4.

Taking the increasing logarithm base phi yields L>11/4. Therefore the right
side of (C4) is positive, and (C2) forces delta>6.

On the other hand phi³=2phi+1>4, so L<3 and A<7. Condition (C3) gives
delta<7. There is no integer strictly between 6 and 7.

**ell_G=1 is exactly the original sharp case.** The reviewed prospective
theorem proves the simultaneous conditions if and only if

    delta_star=(1+D2)/(q2-q1) < delta < A,
    4<delta_star<5, 6<A<7.

The only integers in this interval are 5 and 6. Its analytical Gibbs bound
excludes every k>=3, not only a finite prefix of competitors:

    S_k-S_2 >= g(delta)
       = 1-log_phi(p+p² phi^delta)-D2+delta q2 > 1/2.

For completeness, the threshold bounds have exact elementary proofs.
First, delta_star>4 is equivalent to p²L>1. One has p²>3/8 and L>8/3,
the latter from phi^8=21phi+13<64. Second,

    5(q2-q1)-(1+D2)
      =4-5p²+q2(1-L)>7-11p>0,

because L<3. Finally phi²<4<phi³ implies 2<L<3, hence 6<A<7.
The all-k Gibbs argument is supplied by the linked theorem, and does not
depend on any numerical grid in delta or k.
