 H_j^{(h)}(x,y)
   &=-\lambda\|\Psi'(\tau_j)\|^2(x^2+y^2)
       +W_h(\tau_j,x,y),\\
 K_a(u_j,x,y)
   &=\frac12\left[
       H_{j+1}^{(h)}(x,y)
       -H_{j+1}^{(h)}((x,y)-h\hat a)\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{S3}
\]
Thus the source is a function of the actual recorded material increments:
\[
 \|D_u^+\Psi_j^{(h)}\|^2
   =\|\Psi'(\tau_{j+1})\|^2,\qquad
 \Delta_h^\perp H_{j+1}^{(h)}
   =-4\lambda\|D_u^+\Psi_j^{(h)}\|^2.
\tag{S4}
\]
The identities \(\Delta_h^\perp(x^2+y^2)=4\) and (S2) prove the second
equality exactly. The owned independent-variation classification then sets
all 16 coframe, 24 Lorentz-link and two scalar/top Euler rows to zero at
every free interior site. No boundary Euler row is silently added.

This algebraic construction is exact at every mesh admitted by the stated
CAR chart. If the continuum field has fixed strict slack
\(\sup_I\|\Psi\|<\sqrt{1-p}\), the estimate
\[
 \max_j\|\Psi_j^{(h)}-\Psi(u_j)\|
 \le \frac{T h^2}{24}\sup_I\|\Psi'''\|,
\tag{S5}
\]
where \(T\) is the elapsed interval length, admits all sufficiently fine
meshes. The stronger condition
\[
 \|\Psi(u_0)\|+T\sup_I\|\Psi'\|<\sqrt{1-p}
\tag{S6}
\]
admits every coarse mesh as well. Small amplitude alone, without a
derivative or mesh condition, does not control all coarse midpoint sums.

The fixed continuum target is
\[
 H(t,x,y)=-\lambda\|\Psi'(t)\|^2(x^2+y^2)+W(t,x,y),
 \qquad
 g=2\,du\,dv+H\,du^2-dx^2-dy^2.
\tag{S7}
\]
It satisfies the full Einstein-null-scalar equations with the same
\(\lambda\), as established above. For general \(W\), its native lift
approximates this fixed metric at order \(h^2\); exact sampling is the
separate common-solution subclass of §8. In both cases the finite native
Euler equations themselves vanish exactly.

### 9.2. Uniform interior difference estimates

Write \(S_hW\) for sampling and \(e_h=W_h-S_hW\). Then
\[
 \Delta_h^\perp e_h=f_h:=-\Delta_h^\perp S_hW,\qquad
 e_h=0\quad\hbox{on the boundary}.
\tag{S8}
\]
Let \(D_h^\beta\) denote any product of transverse forward differences.
Taylor's integral remainder and \(\Delta_\perp W=0\) give, wherever the
complete stencil lies in the outer rectangle,
\[
 \|D_h^\beta\partial_t^a f_h\|_\infty
 \le C h^2\|W\|_{C^{a+|\beta|+4}}.
\tag{S9}
\]
The one-dimensional estimate used here is
\[
 \left|\frac{w(x+h)-2w(x)+w(x-h)}{h^2}-w''(x)\right|
 \le\frac{h^2}{12}\sup_{[x-h,x+h]}|w^{(4)}|.
\tag{S10}
\]
Differences commute with translations and derivatives, so applying (S10)
to \(D_h^\beta\partial_t^aW\) proves (S9) without an artificial
\(h^{-|\beta|}\) loss.

The explicit barrier
\[
 B(x,y)=\frac{x(1-x)+y(1-y)}4,\qquad
 \Delta_h^\perp B=-1,\quad B\ge0,\quad \max B=\frac18
\tag{S11}
\]
and discrete comparison first give
\[
 \|\partial_t^a e_h\|_\infty
 \le\frac18\|\partial_t^a f_h\|_\infty=O(h^2).
\tag{S12}
\]
For example,
\[
 \|e_h\|_\infty
 \le \frac{h^2}{96}
       \big(\|W_{xxxx}\|_\infty+\|W_{yyyy}\|_\infty\big).
\tag{S13}
\]

The derivative upgrade follows from three elementary estimates for the
actual grid operator.

**Discrete energy estimate.** Use
\(\|v\|_{2,h}^2=h^2\sum_z|v(z)|^2\). If
\(\Delta_h^\perp v=f\) and \(\eta\) is a fixed smooth cutoff supported
where the equation holds, summation by parts and the exact edge identity
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (v_y-v_x)(\eta_y^2v_y-\eta_x^2v_x)
 =(\eta_yv_y-\eta_xv_x)^2
   -v_xv_y(\eta_y-\eta_x)^2
\end{aligned}
\tag{S14}
\]
bound the cutoff energy by
\(\|f\|_{2,h}\|v\|_{2,h}+C_\eta\|v\|_{2,h}^2\).
Consequently, on nested fixed interior rectangles,
\[
 \|D_hv\|_{2,h;\Omega_1}
 \le C_{\Omega_1,\Omega_2}
   \big(\|v\|_{2,h;\Omega_2}+\|f\|_{2,h;\Omega_2}\big).
\tag{S15}
\]
The constant is independent of \(h\) when the cutoff stencil fits inside
\(\Omega_2\).

**Commuting differences.** The constant coefficient operator commutes
exactly with forward differences. Applying (S15) repeatedly on a finite
nested chain of interior rectangles gives, for every fixed \(m\ge1\),
\[
 \sum_{|\alpha|\le m}\|D_h^\alpha v\|_{2,h;\Omega_1}
 \le C_m\left[
    \|v\|_{2,h;\Omega_2}
    +\sum_{|\beta|\le m-1}
        \|D_h^\beta f\|_{2,h;\Omega_2}\right].
\tag{S16}
\]
Each step retains room for all translated stencils. A fixed derivative
order requires only that \(h\) be below a fixed fraction of the distance
to the outer boundary. No inverse estimate with a growing power of \(1/h\)
is used.

**Uniform discrete Sobolev estimate.** For a finitely supported function
on \(h\mathbb Z^2\), use
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \widehat v_h(\xi)
   &=h^2\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z^2}v(hn)e^{-ihn\cdot\xi},\\
 v(hn)
   &=(2\pi)^{-2}\int_{\mathcal B_h}
                 \widehat v_h(\xi)e^{ihn\cdot\xi}\,d\xi,\\
 \|v\|_{2,h}^2
   &=(2\pi)^{-2}\int_{\mathcal B_h}|\widehat v_h(\xi)|^2\,d\xi,
 \qquad \mathcal B_h=[-\pi/h,\pi/h]^2 .
\end{aligned}
\tag{S17}
\]
The symbol of \(-\Delta_h^\perp\) is
\[
 s_h(\xi)=\frac4{h^2}
   \left[\sin^2\frac{h\xi_x}2+\sin^2\frac{h\xi_y}2\right]
 \ge\frac4{\pi^2}|\xi|^2 .
\tag{S18}
\]
Hence \(\int_{\mathcal B_h}(1+s_h)^{-2}\,d\xi\) is bounded uniformly
in \(h\). Fourier inversion and weighted Cauchy--Schwarz prove
\[
 \|v\|_\infty\le C\|(I-\Delta_h^\perp)v\|_{2,h},
\tag{S19}
\]
or the corresponding bound by the \(2,h\) norms of \(v\) and its second
forward differences.

Apply (S19) to a cutoff times
\(D_h^\alpha\partial_t^a e_h\), and (S16) through order
\(|\alpha|+2\). Derivatives of the fixed cutoff are uniformly bounded.
The required \(f_h\) differences have order at most \(|\alpha|+1\);
their bounds in (S9) use derivatives of \(W\) through
\(a+|\alpha|+5\). Thus
\[
 \boxed{
 \max_{a+|\alpha|\le r}
 \|D_h^\alpha\partial_t^a e_h\|_{\infty;J\times K_h}
 \le C_{r,J,K,W}h^2 .}
\tag{S20}
\]
This is the missing uniform estimate for the actual Dirichlet solution.
The constant may depend on fixed derivative bounds and the fixed interior
margin, but never on the mesh.

### 9.3. Smooth reconstruction using only the finite coframe and CAR records

An estimate for a grid function is not by itself a \(C^r\) statement.
Fix \(m=r+1\), and choose a smooth bump \(\rho\) on \(\mathbb R\) with
\(\rho(s)>0\) for \(|s|<1\) and \(\rho(s)=0\) for \(|s|\ge1\). Define
\[
 \phi(s)=\frac{\rho(s)}
               {\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\rho(s-k)}.
\tag{S21}
\]
The denominator is positive,
\(\sum_i\phi(s-i)=1\), and \(\phi(k)=\delta_{k0}\) at integers.
Let \(P_if\) be the degree-\(m\) polynomial interpolating
\(f_i,\ldots,f_{i+m}\) at their mesh nodes. Set
\[
 \mathcal I_h f(x)=
       \sum_i\phi(x/h-i)P_if(x).
\tag{S22}
\]
Translation gives the same construction on a shifted grid. On a fixed
compact interior set it uses only a fixed number of nearby records for
sufficiently small \(h\). It is smooth, reproduces the actual node values,
and reproduces polynomials of degree at most \(m\). Tensor it in the
needed coordinates.

Derivative stability follows from polynomial reproduction, rather than
from a crude \(h^{-r}\) bound on node values. On one mesh cell an
order-\(a\) derivative is a finite weighted combination of \(h^{-a}f_n\).
Use local node indices relative to the reference cell. The coefficient sequence annihilates polynomials of degree below \(a\).
Its finite generating Laurent polynomial is therefore divisible by
\((z-1)^a\). Factoring this polynomial rewrites the derivative as a
bounded finite combination of \(D_h^af\). The coefficients and their
factored versions are uniformly bounded on the reference cell.
Tensoring yields
\[
 \|\partial^\alpha\mathcal I_hF\|_\infty
 \le C_{m,\alpha}\|D_h^\alpha F\|_\infty
\tag{S23}
\]
on a slightly enlarged compact set, including mixed time/space
derivatives. Taylor expansion through total degree \(|\alpha|+1\)
and polynomial reproduction also give the consistency estimate
\[
 \|\mathcal I_hS_hF-F\|_{C^r}
 \le Ch^2\|F\|_{C^{r+2}}.
\tag{S24}
\]

For the metric, use the tensor operator
\(\mathcal I_h^{\tau,x,y}\) on the **actual finite coframe records**,
with the shifted temporal grid \(\tau_j=jh-h/2\):
\[
 \widetilde H_h
   =\mathcal I_h^{\tau,x,y}\big(H_j^{(h)}|_{\mathrm{grid}}\big),
 \qquad
 \widetilde g_h
   =2\,du\,dv+\widetilde H_h\,du^2-dx^2-dy^2 .
\tag{S25}
\]
This readout does not evaluate the target continuum fields between time
nodes. The smoothly parameterized \(W_h(t)\) in the proof is only an
estimation device. Indeed
\[
 \frac{F(t+h)-F(t)}h
    =\int_0^1F'(t+sh)\,ds
\tag{S26}
\]
and its iteration convert (S20) to the same bounds for mixed finite
time/space differences of the coframe error
\[
 H_j^{(h)}-S_hH=e_h(\tau_j).
\tag{S27}
\]
Equations (S23)--(S24) then apply. The target \(H\) has the required
\(r+2\) derivatives because (S1) assumes \(r+3\) for \(\Psi\) and
\(r+5\) for \(W\). Consequently
\[
 \boxed{
 \|\widetilde g_h-g\|_{C^r(J\times K)}\le Ch^2,\qquad
 \|\operatorname{Riem}(\widetilde g_h)
       -\operatorname{Riem}(g)\|_{C^{r-2}(J\times K)}
       \le Ch^2 .}
\tag{S28}
\]
The reconstructed metric agrees with every installed coframe value at
its declared readout. Its Brinkmann determinant is fixed and nonzero,
so the nonlinear curvature construction has no degenerating inverse
metric in this estimate.

The matter also has a finite-record-only smooth reconstruction:
\[
 \widetilde\Psi_h
   =\mathcal I_h^u\big(\Psi_j^{(h)}\big),
\tag{S29}
\]
using the unshifted material grid \(u_j\). To prove its derivative
estimate without a formal series, put \(f=\Psi'\) and
\[
 A_hf(t)=\frac1h\int_{-h/2}^{h/2}f(t+s)\,ds,\qquad
 r_h(t)=f(t)-A_hf(t).
\tag{S30}
\]
Taylor's integral identity gives exactly
\[
 A_hf(t)-f(t)
  =\frac1h\int_{-h/2}^{h/2}s^2
      \int_0^1(1-\theta)f''(t+\theta s)\,d\theta\,ds.
\tag{S31}
\]
Its kernel has total mass \(h^2/24\). Differentiation commutes with
this translated integral, and hence
\[
 \|r_h^{(k)}\|_\infty
    \le\frac{h^2}{24}\|\Psi^{(k+3)}\|_\infty.
\tag{S32}
\]
For the actual nodal error
\(\epsilon_j=\Psi_j^{(h)}-\Psi(u_j)\),
\[
 D_u^+\epsilon_j=r_h(u_j+h/2)
\tag{S33}
\]
is exact. Iterating (S26) gives, for \(a\ge1\),
\[
 \|(D_u^+)^a\epsilon\|_\infty
   \le\frac{h^2}{24}\|\Psi^{(a+2)}\|_\infty .
\tag{S34}
\]
The order-zero estimate is the accumulated bound (S5).
Cardinal derivative stability and interpolation consistency now prove
\[
 \boxed{\|\widetilde\Psi_h-\Psi\|_{C^r(J)}\le Ch^2.}
\tag{S35}
\]
The interpolated derivative need not equal the exact native forward
slope at every midpoint. The native slope identity (S4) remains exact;
the smooth derivative readout converges at second order. No inverse of
a global averaging operator is asserted.

Applying the original CAR normalization formula to
\(\widetilde\Psi_h\) reconstructs the full normalized vector. Fixed
strict slack keeps its complementary square root smooth for sufficiently
fine meshes, preserves every recorded vector at \(u_j\), and gives
the same \(C^r\) convergence. These estimates are on fixed compact
interiors, not a uniform assertion through boundary corners.

### 9.4. The actual original connection and plaquette curvature

The native Cartan coefficient satisfies
\[
 \frac{K_x(u_j,x,y)}h
      =\frac12D_x^-H_{j+1}^{(h)}(x,y)
      =\frac12H_x(\tau_{j+1},x-h/2,y)+O(h^2).
\tag{S36}
\]
The first equality is exact. The second follows from (S20) and the
centered first-difference formula for the smooth target. The analogous
statement holds for \(y\).

The original commuting null generators satisfy \(X^3=0\) throughout
their span. Thus the actual based \(u\)-\(x\) face obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_{ux}&=\exp X_{ux},&
 X_{ux}&=K(u_j,x,y)-K(u_j,x+h,y),\\
 \mathcal R(P_{ux})
   &=\frac12(P_{ux}-P_{ux}^{-1})=X_{ux}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{S37}
\]
No exponential curvature term is dropped. Its two coefficient readouts
are exactly

\begin{aligned}
 -\frac2{h^2}[X_{ux}]_{N_2}
    &=D_x^+D_x^-H_{j+1}^{(h)}
      =H_{xx}(\tau_{j+1},x,y)+O(h^2),\\
 -\frac2{h^2}[X_{ux}]_{N_3}

\end{aligned}
\tag{S38}
\]
The \(u\)-\(y\) face gives the corresponding pure and mixed centers.
This proves second-order recovery of the original nonlinear native

The native trace satisfies the configured source equation exactly.

### 9.5. Uniqueness and mesh-uniform stability at fixed finite data

Fix an admitted finite matter record and the actual transverse
Dirichlet values \(g_j\) of \(H\). The original independently varied
equations force
\[
 \Delta_h^\perp H_j=-4\lambda E_{j-1}.
\tag{S39}
\]
The five-point Dirichlet operator is invertible, so \(H\) is unique.
The two original \(N_2,N_3\) Cartan coefficients are then unique by
(S3). This is uniqueness inside the declared Brinkmann/null-link
ansatz; it is not uniqueness among arbitrary metrics or among all
preparation and controller variables.

For two sets of finite data, comparison with (S11) gives on the unit
square
\[
 \boxed{
 \|H-\overline H\|_\infty
 \le\|g-\overline g\|_\infty
       +\frac{|\lambda|}{2}\|E-\overline E\|_\infty .}
\tag{S40}
\]
For the actual material slopes \(v=D_u^+\Psi\),
\[
 |E-\overline E|
   \le(\|v\|+\|\overline v\|)\|v-\overline v\|.
\tag{S41}
\]
The map is therefore uniformly Lipschitz in boundary values and the
native material-slope norm on bounded slope sets. This does not claim
uniform control in the weaker, unscaled nodal-amplitude norm.

The harmonic Dirichlet lift has maximum-norm operator norm one.
For a difference of two harmonic lifts, its forcing is zero; (S15)--(S19)
then bound every fixed-order interior difference by its boundary
maximum norm, with constants independent of \(h\). Thus the inverse
needed for interior recovery does not develop a mesh-dependent
derivative instability.

### 9.6. First and second derivatives of the nonlinear recovery map

Use continuum solution-data coordinates \((\Psi,W)\), with \(W\)
harmonic and the norms in (S1). For a direction \((\eta,V)\), the
exact derivative of the installed field at \(\tau_j\) is
\[
 D H_h[\eta,V]
   =-2\lambda\langle\Psi',\eta'\rangle(x^2+y^2)
       +\mathcal D_hV.
\tag{S42}
\]
For two directions,
\[
 \boxed{
 D^2H_h[(\eta_1,V_1),(\eta_2,V_2)]
    =-2\lambda\langle\eta_1',\eta_2'\rangle(x^2+y^2).}
\tag{S43}
\]
All time evaluations here are at the declared native readout.
The \(W\) lift is linear, so mixed second derivatives involving \(W\)
vanish. All third derivatives of this \(H\) map vanish. The map to
the two scalar/top amplitudes is linear midpoint quadrature; its first
derivative is that quadrature applied to \(\eta\), and its second
derivative is zero.

The full normalized CAR vector also contains
\(N(a)=\sqrt{1-p-\|a\|^2}\). Writing \(s=1-p-\|a\|^2\), its derivatives
are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 DN(a)[b]
   &=-\frac{\langle a,b\rangle}{\sqrt s},\\
 D^2N(a)[b_1,b_2]
   &=-\frac{\langle b_1,b_2\rangle}{\sqrt s}
     -\frac{\langle a,b_1\rangle\langle a,b_2\rangle}{s^{3/2}} .
\end{aligned}
\tag{S44}
\]
Fixed strict slack bounds these derivatives and all derivatives needed
for their \(C^r\) estimates. The full normalized state's second
derivative is not declared zero.

Apply the finite-record cardinal readout to (S42)--(S43). The continuum
map has the same formulas with \(\mathcal D_hV\) replaced by \(V\).
Equations (S20), (S23)--(S24), and (S31)--(S34), also applied to
each direction \(\eta\), give on bounded data sets
\[
 \|D^k\widetilde{\mathscr R}_h-D^k\mathscr R\|
       \le Ch^2,\qquad k=0,1,2,
\tag{S45}
\]
where \(\mathscr R\) is the solution map to \((H,\Psi)\),
\(\widetilde{\mathscr R}_h\) is its actual finite-record readout, the
output norms are \(C^r(J\times K)\times C^r(J)\), and for \(k\ge1\)
the left side is the corresponding operator norm on the data spaces
in (S1). For \(k=0\) it is the value difference. Composition with
(S44) gives the same conclusion for the normalized CAR vector.

The second derivative (S43) is exact at native sampled times, including
after transverse polynomial interpolation, because that interpolation
reproduces \(x^2+y^2\). Between sampled times its time interpolation
has an \(O(h^2)\) error; arbitrary smooth time coefficients are not
claimed to be reproduced exactly.

Likewise the original \(K/h\) and \(\mathcal R(P)/h^2\) have uniformly
bounded data derivatives. Their harmonic first-derivative errors are
\(O(h^2)\). Their second data derivatives are exact at the native times
and proper component centers, because centered first and second
differences of the quadratic radial term are exact. A continuous
time readout retains the \(O(h^2)\) interpolation error. This statement
concerns the original connection coefficients and odd plaquette
curvature; it does not identify derivatives of \(\exp K\) with those
of \(K\).

Thus the nonlinear recovery map is \(C^2\) in its solution data with
uniform refinement bounds. These are **derivatives in solution data**,
not the unrestricted action Hessian, and they do not prove a strict
minimum under arbitrary native physical perturbations. Full action,
preparation, clock and admission obligations retain their previous scope.

### 9.7. Sector soundness under convergent data

Uniqueness also gives the reverse implication within this sector.
Consider any exact finite roots on the same ansatz and Dirichlet
carrier. Let \(v_h(t)\) be a declared smooth interpolation of their
actual material slopes, with
\[
 v_h(\tau_j)=D_u^+\Psi^{(h)}_{j-1}.
\tag{S46}
\]
Let \(g_h(t,s)\) interpolate their actual \(H\) boundary data at the
coframe times, and define the associated harmonic boundary data
\[
 w_h(t,s)
   =g_h(t,s)+\lambda\|v_h(t)\|^2(x^2+y^2).
\tag{S47}
\]
Suppose \(v_h\to\Psi'\) in \(C^r\) time and \(w_h\to W\) at the
boundary grid sites in the maximum norm together with time derivatives
through order \(r\). Let their combined discrepancy be
\(\varepsilon_h\to0\). Assume the limiting fields satisfy (S1), and
the initial matter-record discrepancy \(b_h\) tends to zero.

At every actual node, uniqueness expresses \(H_h\) as its radial
particular solution plus the harmonic lift of \(w_h\). Subtract the
lift of the exact sampled \(W\) boundary. The difference is discretely
harmonic, with boundary size at most \(\varepsilon_h\), also after
the stated time differentiations. Maximum comparison and the
homogeneous form of the interior estimates bound every fixed interior
spatial difference by \(C\varepsilon_h\). Equation (S26) supplies
the mixed time-difference bounds needed for the finite-record
interpolant. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|\widetilde g_h-g\|_{C^r(J\times K)}
     &\le C(h^2+\varepsilon_h),\\
 \|\widetilde\Psi_h-\Psi\|_{C^r(J)}
     &\le C(h^2+\varepsilon_h+b_h).
\end{aligned}
\tag{S48}
\]
For the second estimate, the first difference of the material error
is the sampled slope discrepancy plus the exact remainder \(r_h\)
in (S33). Its higher differences are controlled by the assumed time
derivatives and (S32); its value error is at most
\[
 b_h+T\varepsilon_h+\frac{T h^2}{24}\sup_I\|\Psi'''\|.
\tag{S49}
\]
Cardinal stability then proves the second line of (S48).
The original centered \(K/h\) and normalized plaquette curvatures
have the corresponding \(O(h^2+\varepsilon_h)\) errors.

Every such regular limit satisfies the full Einstein-null-scalar
equations with the same \(\lambda\). This is two-sided solution
correspondence for the configured Brinkmann sector with explicit
data and regularity hypotheses. Uncontrolled time oscillations or
changing boundary problems are not asserted to converge automatically.
No general-metric, raw-216, or full D0 physical-selection theorem is
inferred.

For a local action argument requiring the sourced equation at both
\(z\) and \(z-\hat a\), solve on the outer \(\Omega^+\) and restrict
free variations to a fixed inner region whose **entire expanded
stencil lies inside the outer free solver region**. This is compatible
with all compact-interior estimates above. It does not impose a
sourced equation at a fixed Dirichlet boundary.

## 10. An exact finite curvature-square correction preserves the sourced roots

The result below addresses action nonuniqueness on the actual finite carrier.
It uses the same four-dimensional coframe and the same six based plaquettes;
it does not replace the finite curvature by a continuum tensor.

### 10.1. Functional, variation domain and exact conclusion

Write the dimensionless coframe as e=v/h, set g=e^T eta e, and use the
original proper-Lorentz matrix trace. Define all ordered components by
\[
F_{rs}(z)=h^{-2}\mathcal R(P_{rs}(z)),\quad
F_{sr}=-F_{rs},\quad F_{rr}=0,
\]
and the explicit local functional
\[
Q_h[e,U]=\frac{h^4}{4}\sum_z\det(e_z)
 \sum_{r,s,t,w=0}^3g^{rt}(z)g^{sw}(z)
                 \operatorname{tr}[F_{rs}(z)F_{tw}(z)].
\tag{29}
\]
The factor 1/4 refers to the full ordered index sums displayed here.
Every face is based at z, so a local proper-Lorentz frame change conjugates
both factors by the same matrix. The trace, metric and determinant make
(29) gauge invariant. No trace using a positive observer adjoint is substituted.

Use compactly supported independent coframe/link variations for which the
**entire expanded stencil** is inside a region satisfying the original
sourced equations. Equivalently, solve on an outer rectangle and put all
free variations inside a fixed smaller rectangle, as in section 9. The
time/v collar also contains the full stencil. These conditions matter:
a fixed Dirichlet boundary need not itself satisfy the sourced equation.

**Theorem.** On every admitted original sourced Brinkmann root in that
region, all sixteen coframe and twenty-four proper-Lorentz first-source
components of Q_h vanish exactly; Q_h has no scalar/top matter source.
Consequently, for every fixed real beta (also for a mesh-dependent scalar
beta_h), the same roots solve
\[
D(S_{\mathrm{configured},h}+\beta_h Q_h)=0
\tag{30}
\]
on the stated 40+2 variation domain. The original source ratio lambda and
the exact finite metric/matter records are unchanged. This is preservation
of the exhibited root family and equality of first covectors there.
It is not equality of off-shell operators, all stationary sets or Hessians.

### 10.2. Full first variation before restricting the varied fields

At the background, put K=K_2 N_2+K_3 N_3. The only nonzero faces are
\[
F_{ua}=h^{-2}(1-T_a)K,\qquad a=x,y.
\tag{31}
\]
Because tr(N_a N_b)=0, every trace of two background F components is zero.
Thus Q_h(z)=0 and **all independent coframe derivatives**, including those
of det(e) and both inverse metrics, are zero pointwise with U held fixed.
This statement also transports through the original invertible coframe
dressing by the chain rule.

The inverse metric has g^{uu}=0, g^{uv}=1, g^{vv}=-H and g^{ab}=-delta_ab.
Raising both face indices gives only F^{va}=-F_{ua} and F^{av}=F_{ua}.
In particular g^{vv} supplies no additional term. The exact first variation
in all independent links is therefore
\[
DQ_h=-h^4\sum_{z,a}\operatorname{tr}(F_{ua}\,\delta F_{va}).
\tag{32}
\]
For the original left tangent delta U_r=xi_r U_r, every background link
on a va face is I. The original nonlinear odd curvature has derivative

## 11. Continuum first-source universality of local curvature corrections

There is a broader continuum consequence on the same smooth sourced family.
It is an application of the known almost-universal pp-wave mechanism, not a
new general classification; see [Kuchynka et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.02178),
section IV.2 of the HTML version. The following direct argument fixes the hypotheses used here.

Let Delta_perp H=F(u), with no polynomial or frequency restriction. Consider
compactly supported **unrestricted metric variations** of
\[
S_g=\int\sqrt{-g}\,[c_0+c_1 R+\sum_A c_A I_A],d^4x,
\tag{38}
\]
where coefficients are constant, the sum is finite, and each I_A is a
complete metric contraction of at least two factors of the form
nabla^{m_j} Riem, of arbitrary finite derivative orders. Then
\[
\boxed{\frac1{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_g}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
       =-\tfrac12c_0g_{\mu\nu}+c_1\operatorname{Ric}_{\mu\nu}.}
\tag{39}
\]
Every higher-curvature term in the stated class has zero first metric source.

**Proof.** The only nonzero Christoffel symbols are
Gamma^v_uu=H_u/2, Gamma^v_ui=Gamma^v_iu=H_i/2 and Gamma^i_uu=H_i/2.
Thus du is parallel. Every fully covariant curvature component is a
permutation of R_uiuj. Inductively, each nabla^m Riem is v independent,
has no nonzero component with a lower v index, and has at least two lower
u indices in every nonzero component. Ordinary derivatives preserve this
property. An upper-v Christoffel multiplies a zero tensor component; the
remaining Gamma^i_uu supplies two u labels and cannot lower their count.

A lower u can only be contracted with a lower v, since g^{uu}=g^{ui}=0.
Consequently every rank-two contraction involving two or more curvature
factors or their derivatives vanishes: at least four u indices would have
to remain free. A rank-two contraction linear in nabla^m Riem has both u
indices free, and all other indices transverse. Its nonzero candidates
reduce to ordinary commuting transverse derivatives of H_ij. Pairing them
gives a constant multiple of Delta_perp^{1+m/2} H for even m; odd m cannot
produce a full pairing. For m>=2 this is zero because Delta_perp H=F(u).
For m=0 it is a multiple of Ric. Equivalently, R=0 and Box Ric=0.

Now vary a monomial with k>=2 curvature factors before imposing the metric
form. Variations of volume, metric contractions, derivative connections and
the algebraic-curvature part of delta Riem retain at least k factors after
integration by parts; their rank-two contractions vanish. The two-derivative
delta-g part of delta Riem removes one factor. If k>=3 at least two remain.
If k=2 one remains with at least two derivatives, whose contraction also
vanishes by the preceding argument. Commuting derivatives only creates
already vanishing multiple-curvature terms. This proves (39).

This theorem has the same necessary hostile control: all scalar invariants
also vanish for a generic pp-wave H=x^4, while the Ricci-square Euler tensor
contains Box Ric, here a nonzero uu component. Vanishing scalar densities
alone never established (39).

Derivative-only scalar actions have a compatible limited extension. For
X_AB=g^{mu nu} partial_mu Psi_A partial_nu Psi_B and a smooth function P(X),
X=0 on Psi(u). Its metric source depends only on P(0) and the matrix
P_X(0), and its scalar equations vanish by v-independence. Higher powers
of X do not change these first equations. This does not include a general
potential or a coupling f(Psi)R: the latter has an additional
-(nabla_mu nabla_nu f)=-f''(u)(du)_mu(du)_nu contribution.

For (38) with vanishing net cosmological term and the actual required
gravity/matter first-jet ratio, there is therefore no need to determine
every higher-curvature coefficient to preserve this sourced continuum
family. This is a precise conditional application, not a proof that the
actual complete D0 price is a local metric action of this form. It also
does not lift arbitrary continuum corrections to exact finite actions;
section 10 proves that separate finite statement for its specific Q_h.
Analytic infinite-derivative examples are discussed by
[Kolar, Malek and Mazumdar, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08555), section III;
applying them requires well-defined operators and justified first
variation on the Ricci zero mode. No inverse-Box or general nonlocal D0
functional is covered by (38).
