s=\frac{R^2}{d^2B^2},\quad \frac1{4d^2}<s\le\frac1{d^2}.}
\tag{1}

## 3. Quantitative strengthening: common R has polynomial BIT length

For ANY finite family of \(A\) real \(d\times d\) orthogonal targets and \(0<\varepsilon<1\), one can choose EXACTLY orthogonal rational approximants \(\widehat U_a\) with
\[
\max_a\|\widehat U_a-U_a\|_{\rm op}\le\varepsilon
\]
and a single denominator satisfying
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{bitlen}(R)\le
1+A\,d(d-1)\left[1+2\log_2\left\lceil\frac{2d(d-1)}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil\right].}
\tag{3}
\]

**Proof.** Givens elimination expresses an arbitrary orthogonal matrix in at most \(d(d-1)/2\) planar rotations and exact signs. Split each angle into two, obtaining at most \(m=d(d-1)\) planar factors with half-angle tangent \(r\in[-1,1]\). For \(Q=\lceil2m/\varepsilon\rceil\), round \(r\) to \(k/Q\). The exact rational rotation has entries
\[
c=\frac{Q^2-k^2}{Q^2+k^2},\quad
s=\frac{2Qk}{Q^2+k^2},\quad c^2+s^2=1.
\]
The plane-rotation map is at most 2-Lipschitz in \(r\). Nearest-grid rounding gives per-factor operator error at most \(1/Q\), hence the product error is at most \(m/Q\le\varepsilon/2\). The denominator of each factor is at most \(2Q^2\). Let \(R\) be the product of all such denominators for all addresses. Each full matrix entry has denominator dividing \(R\), so \(\log_2R\le A m(1+2\log_2Q)\), which proves (3). This is a general exact finite theorem; no numerical approximation of the final orthogonality is used.

The fixed-width table uses at most \(A d^2(\operatorname{bitlen}(R)+2)\) bits and admits a direct reversible equality/lookup/comparator circuit of polynomial logical size in \(A,d,\log(1/\varepsilon)\). The VALUE of \(R\) may be enormous, but the literal bit width is polynomial. Equation (1) shows the herald readiness is independent of \(A\) and precision.
